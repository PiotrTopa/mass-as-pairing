# I.2 — Hasenbusch split + multiple-time-scale integrator exact

**Statement.** The Hasenbusch-split RHMC with nested time scales (`masspairing.action.HasenbuschModel`,
`masspairing.hmc.MTSHMC`) simulates the same measure as the plain RHMC of I.1. The split is
det(D†D)^{1/2} = Π_k det[(D†D + s_{k−1})/(D†D + s_k)]^{1/2} · det(D†D + s_n)^{1/2}, 0 = s_0 < s_1 < … < s_n
(D†D + μ² = (D + μ)†(D + μ) because D† = −D), one pseudofermion per factor, actions and heat baths by Lanczos at the
tolerances of the plain RHMC (so the accept/reject is exact), forces by per-term Zolotarev partial fractions, nested
PQPQP Omelyan over the levels. (A) Every term's heat bath and action agree with dense linear algebra, the term
determinants multiply to det(D†D)^{1/2}, each force is the gradient of its action, the nested integrator is reversible.
(B) ΔH ∝ δτ² across the levels; a wrong pole set in the lightest force destroys the scaling. (C) On 2⁴ the Hasenbusch/MTS
chain agrees with the exact-determinant Metropolis reference at y = 1.2 and 2.5, and simulating only the heavy factor is
rejected. (D) On 4⁴ at P_c the plain and the Hasenbusch/MTS production settings give the same expectation values.

**Method.** (A) dense eigen-decomposition of D†D on 4⁴ against the Lanczos heat bath (t ↦ ((t + a)/(t + b))^{1/4},
(t + s_n)^{1/4}) and action of every term; finite differences of the exact term actions; forward–flip–backward
integration. (B) four level settings and the sabotaged force from one thermalised configuration (seed 62). (C) the
Metropolis reference of I.1 (single-site, exact dense determinant) at y = 1.2 (shared with I.1) and y = 2.5; at y = 1.2
the determinant changes ⟨σ²⟩ only at the per-cent level, at y = 2.5 it matters more, which is where the sabotage is run.
The runs are generated by `scripts/derive_k7.py mc` (≈ 11 min for the I.2 runs) and stored in `data/derived/k7/mc/`; the
check regenerates a prefix of each (200 sweeps, 40 trajectories after the 200 thermalisation trajectories) and requires
bit identity. (D) stored production-setting chains with dense (exact) fermion measurements every trajectory; 20-block
errors.

**Controls.** (B) lightest-term poles × 3: ⟨|ΔH|⟩ 0.0932 vs 0.0004 (rejected: > 10×). (C) dropping the ratio terms
(simulating det(D†D + 3)^{1/2}): largest pull 6.9σ (rejected: > 5σ).
